Why this decomposition

Driven by one mechanical fact and one judgement. The fact: builders run in parallel and pieces must be file-disjoint. Seven concerns touch public/index.html (OPS_ECON, OP_LABELS, the duplicated opNiceName map, _opAfterFound, the new window.MythicTransportBridge block, _ppGenListing's haul roll, the launcher tile, the script tag). They collapse into ONE piece (8), which also owns version.txt and sw.js because CLAUDE.md's deploy triple must move together or the update check breaks. Piece 8 is large but unavoidable, and it has the strongest available bar: index.html's own exemplar hunks. The judgement: seams fall where a critic can judge the artifact WITHOUT the rest of the feature. Each remaining piece is one file with a type-matched exemplar from this repo's ratified best work, so a blind A/B is meaningful — catalog vs catalog (rigs.data.js vs production.data.js), bridge vs bridge, pure-and-total vs tiers.js, renderer vs production.render.js, money/failure-envelope vs cost.js, derived-state vs production.state.js, migration vs sql/015. I deliberately did NOT bar contracts.js against community.api.js nor index.js against city/index.js even though those are the closer functional analogues: neither is in the ratified BAR list, and cost.js / community.bridge.js test the properties that actually matter here (atomic spend + refund closure; NULL_BRIDGE key-for-key parity). It also produces pieces judgeable only in context ("step 3" is half a legacy hunk plus half a catalog). REJECTED DECOMPOSITION #2 — one "module" piece for all of public/src/transport. It has no type-matched bar left in the ratified list once the other six are assigned, and it is thin glue over pinned signatures.
